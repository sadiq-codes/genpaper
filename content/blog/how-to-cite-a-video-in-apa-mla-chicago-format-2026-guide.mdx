## Why Citing Videos Matters

Video content is increasingly used in academic research. Whether you are referencing a YouTube tutorial, a documentary, or a lecture recording, proper citation:

- **Gives credit** to the original creator
- **Allows readers** to verify your sources
- **Demonstrates** the breadth of your research
- **Avoids plagiarism** and academic misconduct

Different citation styles have specific rules for video sources. Let us break down each format.

## How to Cite a Video in APA Format

APA 7th edition treats videos as audiovisual media. The basic format depends on where the video is hosted.

### APA Video Citation Format (Reference List)

**Basic template:**

Author, A. A. [Username]. (Year, Month Day). *Title of video* [Video]. Platform. URL

**Key elements:**

- **Author**: The person or organization that uploaded the video
- **Username**: Include in brackets if different from author name
- **Date**: Full date of upload (Year, Month Day)
- **Title**: In italics, sentence case
- **Format**: [Video] in brackets
- **Platform**: YouTube, Vimeo, TED, etc.
- **URL**: Direct link to the video

### APA YouTube Video Example

**Reference list:**

TED. (2019, April 23). *The danger of a single story | Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie* [Video]. YouTube. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9Ihs241zeg

**In-text citation:**

(TED, 2019)

or

TED (2019) discusses how single narratives can limit understanding.

### APA Video In-Text Citation Rules

For in-text citations, use the author (or username) and year:

- **(Author, Year)** – Parenthetical
- **Author (Year)** – Narrative

If you reference a specific moment, include the timestamp:

(TED, 2019, 4:32)

## How to Cite a Video in MLA Format

MLA 9th edition uses a flexible container system for citing videos.

### MLA Video Citation Format (Works Cited)

**Basic template:**

"Title of Video." *Website Name*, uploaded by Author/Username, Day Month Year, URL.

**Key elements:**

- **Title**: In quotation marks
- **Website**: Platform name in italics
- **Uploaded by**: The channel or person who posted it
- **Date**: Day Month Year format
- **URL**: Without https://

### MLA YouTube Video Example

**Works Cited:**

"The Danger of a Single Story." *YouTube*, uploaded by TED, 7 Oct. 2009, www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9Ihs241zeg.

**In-text citation:**

("Danger of a Single Story" 00:04:32)

or

In "The Danger of a Single Story," Adichie explains the impact of incomplete narratives.

### MLA Video In-Text Citation Rules

Use a shortened title in quotation marks if no clear author:

- **("Title" Timestamp)** – Parenthetical
- **"Title"** – Narrative with context

## How to Cite a Video in Chicago Format

Chicago style offers two systems: Notes-Bibliography and Author-Date. Most humanities courses use Notes-Bibliography.

### Chicago Notes-Bibliography Format

**Footnote:**

1. Author, "Title of Video," Platform, Month Day, Year, video, duration, URL.

**Bibliography:**

Author. "Title of Video." Platform. Month Day, Year. Video, duration. URL.

### Chicago YouTube Video Example

**Footnote:**

1. TED, "The Danger of a Single Story | Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie," YouTube, October 7, 2009, video, 19:16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9Ihs241zeg.

**Bibliography:**

TED. "The Danger of a Single Story | Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie." YouTube. October 7, 2009. Video, 19:16. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9Ihs241zeg.

### Chicago Author-Date Format

For sciences and social sciences using Author-Date:

**Reference list:**

TED. 2009. "The Danger of a Single Story | Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie." YouTube, video, 19:16. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9Ihs241zeg.

**In-text:**

(TED 2009)

## How to Cite YouTube Videos

YouTube is the most common video source in academic papers. Here is a quick reference for all three formats:

### YouTube Citation Comparison Table

**APA 7:**
- Channel Name. (Year, Month Day). *Title* [Video]. YouTube. URL

**MLA 9:**
- "Title." *YouTube*, uploaded by Channel, Day Month Year, URL.

**Chicago:**
- Channel. "Title." YouTube. Month Day, Year. Video, duration. URL.

### Tips for Citing YouTube Videos

1. **Use the channel name** as the author unless an individual is clearly identified
2. **Include the upload date**, not when you accessed it (except in Chicago)
3. **Copy the URL** directly from the browser (not the share link for consistency)
4. **Add timestamps** when referencing specific moments

## How to Cite TED Talks

TED Talks require special attention because they appear on multiple platforms.

### Citing TED Talks from ted.com

**APA:**

Adichie, C. N. (2009, July). *The danger of a single story* [Video]. TED Conferences. https://www.ted.com/talks/chimamanda_ngozi_adichie_the_danger_of_a_single_story

**MLA:**

Adichie, Chimamanda Ngozi. "The Danger of a Single Story." *TED*, July 2009, www.ted.com/talks/chimamanda_ngozi_adichie_the_danger_of_a_single_story.

**Chicago:**

Adichie, Chimamanda Ngozi. "The Danger of a Single Story." TED. July 2009. Video, 18:49. https://www.ted.com/talks/chimamanda_ngozi_adichie_the_danger_of_a_single_story.

### TED Talk vs YouTube Version

If the TED Talk is available on both ted.com and YouTube:

- **Prefer the official TED website** when available
- **Use the speaker as author** for TED website citations
- **Use "TED" as author** for YouTube versions

## How to Cite Films and Documentaries

Films and documentaries follow slightly different rules than online videos.

### Film Citation Format by Style

**APA (streaming):**

Director, D. D. (Director). (Year). *Title of film* [Film]. Production Company.

or for streaming:

Director, D. D. (Director). (Year). *Title of film* [Film; Streaming service version]. Production Company.

**MLA:**

*Title of Film*. Directed by Director Name, Production Company, Year.

**Chicago:**

*Title of Film*. Directed by Director Name. Production Company, Year.

### Documentary Example

**APA:**

Guggenheim, D. (Director). (2006). *An inconvenient truth* [Film]. Paramount Classics.

**MLA:**

*An Inconvenient Truth*. Directed by Davis Guggenheim, Paramount Classics, 2006.

**Chicago:**

*An Inconvenient Truth*. Directed by Davis Guggenheim. Paramount Classics, 2006.

## Common Video Citation Mistakes

Avoid these frequent errors when citing videos:

### Mistake 1: Missing the Upload Date

**Wrong:** TED. *The danger of a single story* [Video]. YouTube. URL

**Right:** TED. (2009, October 7). *The danger of a single story* [Video]. YouTube. URL

### Mistake 2: Using Access Date Instead of Upload Date

Citation styles require the upload or publication date, not when you watched it.

### Mistake 3: Forgetting the [Video] Format Label

APA requires you to identify the medium in brackets. Without it, readers cannot identify the source type.

### Mistake 4: Using Shortened URLs

Always use the full URL, not shortened links like youtu.be.

### Mistake 5: Wrong Capitalization

- **APA**: Sentence case for titles
- **MLA**: Title case with quotation marks
- **Chicago**: Title case with quotation marks

## FAQ

### How do I cite a video with no author?

Start the citation with the video title. In APA, move the title to the author position. In MLA, begin with the title in quotation marks.

### Do I include the video length in my citation?

Chicago format requires the duration. APA and MLA do not require it but you may include it for clarity.

### How do I cite a specific timestamp in a video?

Add the timestamp in parentheses after the year:
- APA: (Author, Year, 2:30)
- MLA: ("Title" 00:02:30)
- Chicago: Include in footnote

### Can I cite Netflix or streaming videos?

Yes. Treat streaming videos like films but include the streaming platform. Add [Film; Netflix version] in APA format.

### How do I cite a video lecture from my professor?

Use the instructor name as author and the course platform as the source. Include [Video lecture] as the format in APA.

## Conclusion

Citing videos correctly demonstrates your research skills and protects you from plagiarism. Remember these key points:

- **Identify the platform** (YouTube, TED, streaming service)
- **Use the correct date format** for your citation style
- **Include all required elements** (author, title, date, URL)
- **Add timestamps** when referencing specific moments

With practice, citing videos becomes second nature.
