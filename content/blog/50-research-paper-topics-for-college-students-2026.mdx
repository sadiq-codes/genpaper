## How to Choose a Research Paper Topic

Before diving into the list, here's a quick framework for picking the right topic:

**1. Check your assignment requirements**
Does your professor want a specific type of paper? Argumentative, analytical, expository? Make sure your topic fits the assignment.

**2. Pick something you're curious about**
You'll spend hours researching this. Choose a topic that genuinely interests you—it shows in your writing.

**3. Make sure sources exist**
A great topic idea is useless if you can't find credible sources. Do a quick search before committing.

**4. Narrow it down**
"Climate change" is too broad. "How rising sea levels affect coastal cities in Florida" is manageable.

**5. Consider the debate**
The best research papers explore topics with multiple perspectives. Avoid topics that are too one-sided.

## Tips for Narrowing Your Topic

Found a topic you like but it still feels too broad? Here's how to narrow it down:

### Ask the "So What?" Question
Why does this topic matter? Who does it affect? Focusing on specific impacts helps narrow scope.

**Too broad:** Social media and teenagers
**Narrowed:** How Instagram's algorithm changes in 2024 affected teen girls' body image

### Limit by Time, Place, or Population
Add constraints to make your topic manageable.

**Too broad:** The effects of climate change
**Narrowed:** How rising sea levels have affected Miami Beach property values since 2010

### Focus on One Aspect
Big topics have many angles. Pick just one.

**Too broad:** Artificial intelligence in healthcare
**Narrowed:** How AI diagnostic tools are changing radiology workflows in community hospitals

### Consider Your Sources
If you can only find broad overview articles, your topic might be too new or obscure. If you find thousands of results, it's probably too broad.

## FAQ

### How do I know if my research topic is too broad or too narrow?

**Too broad:** You could write a whole book about it. Example: "The history of education."
**Too narrow:** You can barely find 3-5 sources. Example: "How the 2024 lighting renovation in my local library affected study habits."
**Just right:** You can cover it thoroughly in your required page count with available sources.

### Can I use a topic from this list exactly as written?

Yes, but consider adjusting it to fit your specific assignment requirements, your course focus, and your personal interests. Adding a unique angle makes your paper stand out.

### What if my professor rejects my topic?

Ask for specific feedback on why it was rejected. Usually it's too broad, too narrow, not relevant to the course, or lacking a clear argument. Use their feedback to refine rather than starting from scratch.

### How many sources do I need for a research paper?

This depends on your assignment, but a general rule: 1-2 sources per page of content. A 10-page paper typically needs 10-20 sources. Quality matters more than quantity—peer-reviewed sources carry more weight.

### Should I pick a topic I already know about or something new?

There's no wrong answer. A familiar topic means faster research but requires you to find fresh angles. A new topic keeps you engaged but has a steeper learning curve. Choose based on your timeline and interest level.
