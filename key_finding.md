4.0 RESULTS
4.1 Identified Fungal Contaminants
Isolation and identification of fungal contaminants associated with the spoilage of tomatoes was conducted, where Six (6) fungal pathogens were identified based on macroscopic (colony appearance) and microscopic identification with thse help of Standard Mycological Atlas.





 
Table 4.1: Macroscopic and Microscopic Characteristics of Fungal Isolates
Isolate code	Macroscopic	Microscopic	Probable Isolate
Isolate A	Rapidly growing colony displaying black and white colors.	Conidial heads are large globose, dark brown, uniseriate.	Aspergillus Niger
Isolate B	Very fast growing, cottony to fluffy, white to yellow, becoming dark-grey.	Nonstate or sparsely septate, broad, hyphae, and spores	Mucor Spp
Isolate C	These colonies exhibit irregular shapes with greenish-yellow.	Radiate conidial head, hyaline Conidiophore, uniseriate or biseriate.	Aspergillus flavus
Isolate D	White and fluffy, rapidly spreading over the growth medium.	Sporangiospores are smooth-walled, non-septate, simple or branched.	Rhizopus spp
Isolate E	Coloniesare usually fast growing, in shades of green, sometimes white	Fillamentous Hyphae with cinidia, Flasked Shaped Philides arranged in group.	Penicillium spp
Isolate F	Colonies are fast growing, black to olivaceous-black or greyish.	Conidia are ovoid or ellipsoidal. branched acropetal chains (blastocatenate).	Alternaria spp
