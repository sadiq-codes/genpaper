# Literature Review Context

A literature review synthesizes existing scholarship. It does not present original empirical data collection.

## Type Selection

Select the subtype that best matches intent:
- **Type A (Standalone):** Full review paper mapping state of knowledge.
- **Type B (Background Chapter):** Literature chapter supporting a larger thesis/dissertation.
- **Type C (Systematic):** Replicable review with explicit inclusion/exclusion logic.
- **Type D (Scoping):** Broad mapping of concepts/evidence in an emerging area.

## Core Requirements

- Organize around ideas/themes/debates, not one-source-per-paragraph summaries.
- Use synthesis language to compare, integrate, and contrast findings.
- Identify contradictions, methodological limits, and unresolved gaps.
- Explain why influential/landmark studies matter for field development.

## Methodology Meaning (Important)

For literature reviews, "Methodology" means search/selection process, such as:
- Databases and search strategy.
- Inclusion/exclusion logic.
- Time range and screening approach.

Do not define methodology as primary data collection unless original research is explicitly provided.

## Suggested Structure

- **Introduction:** focus, scope, and organizational logic.
- **Body:** thematic/chronological/methodological/theoretical synthesis.
- **Conclusion:** key synthesis, explicit gap statement, and future directions.

## Citation and Evidence Rules

- Most substantive claims should be cited.
- Cite specific findings/theories/methods from sources.
- Do not fabricate statistics or claims.
- If evidence lacks numeric detail, use qualitative phrasing.

## Source Diversity

- Use broad, representative coverage of relevant literature.
- Avoid over-reliance on a small subset of papers.

## Optional Table Patterns

- Summary of studies.
- Comparison of approaches.
- Contradictory findings matrix.

Use tables to support synthesis, not replace analytical prose.
