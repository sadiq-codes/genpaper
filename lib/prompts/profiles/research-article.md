# Research Article Context

A research article advances a clear argument using systematic evidence. It may use primary data, secondary analysis, or both.

## Mode Selection

- **Mode A (Primary Research):** Original data collection and analysis.
- **Mode B (Secondary Analysis):** New argument from existing literature/data/texts.
- **Mode C (Mixed):** Combination of primary and secondary evidence.

## Introduction Standard (CARS)

- Establish territory (importance/context).
- Establish niche (specific gap).
- Occupy niche (question/thesis, approach, contribution, roadmap).

## Section Expectations by Mode

### Mode A (Primary)
- Methodology describes your own data collection/analysis.
- Results report your findings with concrete evidence (stats/themes/interpretive proof).
- Discussion interprets findings and compares with prior work.

### Mode B (Secondary)
- Methodology describes source selection and analytical procedure.
- Results/analysis present your argument using cited evidence.
- Do not fabricate primary empirical outputs.

### Mode C (Mixed)
- Clarify how primary and secondary evidence are integrated.

## Citation Posture by Section

- **Introduction/Lit review:** heavy citation.
- **Methodology:** cite established methods when relevant.
- **Results:** minimal citations for primary results; moderate-heavy for secondary-analysis findings.
- **Discussion:** moderate citation for comparison and theory linkage.
- **Conclusion:** usually light citation.

## Quality Requirements

- Explicit research question or thesis.
- Logical argument progression.
- Claims anchored to evidence.
- Direct handling of counterarguments.
- Clear original contribution statement.

## Evidence Integrity

- Never fabricate citations, statistics, or claims.
- Use quantitative detail only when available from evidence.
- If producing illustrative data, mark it clearly as illustrative in limitations.

## Optional Table Patterns

- Descriptive/analysis results tables.
- Theme matrices for qualitative findings.
- Comparison/synthesis tables for secondary analysis.
